SPEAKER-GROUP SENSITIVITY OF PAIRED DETECTOR COMPARISONS ON ASVSPOOF
2021 DF
Rafaello Virgilli Lucas Alcântara Souza Alexandre Costa Ferro Filho Daniel Tunnermann

ABSTRACT
The gap between XLSR-Mamba’s 1.884% equal-error rate (EER)
and XLSR-Conformer’s 2.273% on ASVspoof 2021 DF depends
strongly on one speaker group. Removing VCC2SM3’s 315 bonafide trials reduces Mamba’s advantage from 0.389 to 0.129 percentage points. Using public scores from eight speech deepfake detectors, we resample trials, observed speakers and spoof attacks,
keeping comparisons paired and refitting EER thresholds. Across
all 28 pairs, bands constructed together for the EER differences exclude zero for 26 pairs under trial resampling and 18 under speaker–
attack resampling. Speaker-only resampling gives the same zeroexclusion decisions. All four self-supervised systems retain their
advantage over the four organizer baselines, with bands excluding
zero for all sixteen comparisons. A limited SpoofCeleb analysis
instead finds that attack-only resampling reproduces the speaker–
attack decisions. These fixed-score results describe sensitivity to
observed groups, without population-coverage claims, causal attribution or a universal resampling prescription. Small EER advantages should therefore be reported alongside their sensitivity to influential evaluation groups.

Index Terms— Audio deepfake detection, paired evaluation, speaker influence, EER
1. INTRODUCTION
On ASVspoof 2021 DF, XLSR-Mamba achieves 1.884%
EER and XLSR-Conformer 2.273%. Deleting VCC2SM3,
which contains 315 bona-fide VCC2018 trials and no spoof
trials, changes the Mamba–Conformer gap from −0.389 to
−0.129 percentage points. Those 315 trials are part of 14,869
bona-fide trials in a benchmark with 533,928 trials overall.
The gap remains negative, but its magnitude depends strongly
on one observed group.
This deletion belongs to a complete family analysis, with
all speakers retained in the principal comparison. Across
eight detectors, we ask which paired EER differences survive changing the resampling unit, and how concentrated
the delete-one speaker influence is in the affected modern
comparisons. ASVspoof 2021 DF has 93 speakers and 110
attacks; a large trial count need not provide broad group
support for a close comparison. Shared group variation may

cancel between detectors, so dependence of individual scores
alone cannot answer this paired question.
Our contribution is an empirical concentration result on
fixed scores: speaker-only resampling reproduces the joint
decisions, and delete-one influence is concentrated to differing degrees across the three affected self-supervised (SSL)
comparisons. We print every primary trial and joint band
and repeat the analysis within the six SSL comparisons. A
limited SpoofCeleb counterexample retains the reason to examine both factors. The reporting consequence is to disclose
influential-group sensitivity when claiming a small paired
EER advantage.
2. RELATED WORK
Benchmark reporting. ASVspoof 2017 reported EER
intervals [1]; ASVspoof 2021 Fig. 4(c) used the Bengio–
Mariéthoz test [2] with Holm correction [3]. Its shared-trial
variant accounts for two systems using the same trials, not
repeated speakers and attacks. We found no uncertainty analysis in the later journal overview [4] or ASVspoof 5 [5]; a
recent SSL benchmark reports point EERs [6]. Other work
reports trial-bootstrap ranks [7], EER spreads [8], corrected
pairwise tests [9], or fixed-threshold intervals [10]. Trainingseed variation also moves rankings [11]; our scores hold
training fixed.
Dependent paired comparison. User-aware biometric
and speech resampling [12, 13], correlation-corrected paired
tests [14], and synchronized resampling for correlated classifiers [15] precede this study. Our speaker and spoof-attack
factors are crossed [16, 17, 18], distinct from the nestedobservation designs studied in [19]. We use these established
tools to measure concentrated group influence on paired EER
comparisons, with every threshold refitted. Recent audiodeepfake work also reports paired and speaker-resampled
EER-difference intervals [20]; here we examine a complete
comparison family and the concentration of speaker-deletion
influence.
Evaluation design. Aggregation weights [21], channel
presentation [22], representations [23], controlled genera-

tor/speaker perturbations [24] and corpus overlap [25] affect
deepfake evaluation. Identity perturbations [26], speakeradversarial training [27] and speaker/synthesizer familiarity
[28] address related sensitivities. Here the scores are fixed
and evaluation weights vary. Simultaneous rank intervals are
established [29]. Because selecting only neighbors in the
observed point-EER ordering uses the outcomes, we include
every detector pair.
3. METHOD
3.1. EER contrast and separation event
Two deterministic systems on a fixed trial list have no sampling uncertainty until a perturbation law is declared. Scores
are oriented so larger means bona fide and sorted in ascending
order. At each ordered position, the false-reject rate (FRR) is
the fraction of bona-fide weight at or below it and the falseaccept rate (FAR) the fraction of spoof weight above it; we
select the first position minimizing their absolute difference
and take their mean as EER, without interpolation. For ordered pair (A, B), ∆EERA,B = 100(EERA − EERB ) percentage points (negative favors A); every replicate refits both
thresholds. A pair is separated under procedure h when its
simultaneous band excludes zero; write this indicator as Ip,h .
3.2. Perturbation laws and scope
Groups use the speaker-ID and attack-ID fields in the official
DF metadata. For converted speech, the speaker ID denotes
the target voice; it need not identify the source recording’s
speaker.
The trial bootstrap independently resamples trials within
class. For SpoofCeleb only, it samples 91,130 trial indices
with replacement from the pooled list, so class counts vary
across replicates. In the speaker×attack product-weight (PW)
bootstrap [18, 30], we independently draw speakers and spoof
attacks uniformly with replacement, drawing as many of each
as observed; a spoof trial receives the product of its speaker
and attack multiplicities, and a bona-fide trial receives only its
speaker multiplicity. Speaker-only fixes attack multiplicities
at one; attack-only fixes speaker and bona-fide trial weights
at one. Each of these four procedures uses 5,000 replicates.
Each replicate’s weights are shared across systems; procedures have separate random streams.
Interpretation. These bands describe sensitivity of fixed
scores to the stated weights, not confidence about future
speakers or attacks. Four VCC spoof strata contain only 4, 4,
4 and 6 speakers. Coverage results for crossed-array means
do not establish coverage for refitted EER differences [30].
The one-factor, Monte Carlo, mean/sign, pairing, tie and
group-deletion diagnostics were designed after the primary
results and are exploratory. Matching separation decisions
does not decompose variance or identify a causal speaker

effect. A band containing zero establishes neither equality
nor a deployment ranking.

5. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
The contribution is measured concentration across paired
comparisons on this fixed benchmark, not a new confidence
procedure. A diffuse delete-one profile across the affected
SSL pairs would contradict the concentration claim; Table 2
exposes both the strong flagship case and the weaker third
pair. A changed speaker-only decision would contradict the
reported primary factor agreement. Neither finding establishes why those groups matter, and SpoofCeleb shows why
the factor pattern should not become a universal prescription.
For a small paired EER advantage on this benchmark,
report the point difference, trial and group bands for the same
family, and influential-group deletion sensitivity. If only the
trial band excludes zero, describe the advantage as sensitive
to evaluation composition and seek broader group support
before claiming robust superiority. Publish the group memberships and weighting rules needed to reproduce the check.

This study analyzed previously collected ASVspoof 2021 and
SpoofCeleb recordings and detector scores. It involved no
new recording or interaction with human participants; no ethical approval was required. The authors declare no competing
interests.
