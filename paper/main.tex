\begin{abstract}
Audio-deepfake leaderboards rank detectors from repeated trials that share speakers and spoofing attacks. We analyze eight systems on ASVspoof~2021~DF, giving 28 pairwise contrasts. For systems $A$ and $B$, $\Delta\mathrm{EER}_{A,B}=100(\mathrm{EER}_A-\mathrm{EER}_B)$ percentage points; a pair is separated when its 95\% simultaneous max-$t$ band excludes zero. A trial-level bootstrap separates 26/28 pairs and 5/6 organizer-baseline pairs, whereas a speaker$\times$attack product-weight bootstrap separates 18/28 and 0/6. On single-source SpoofCeleb, an analysis archived before dataset access changes the corresponding count from 6/6 to 3/6, showing that between-source mixture changes are not necessary. Twelve fixed positive source/speaker/attack weighting rules reverse 6/12 within-cohort orderings but 0/16 between-cohort orderings. Across the tested perturbations and source deletions, separation between the four SSL detectors and four organizer baselines is stable, whereas their within-cohort ordering is not. These are fixed-score sensitivity bands, not population confidence intervals.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Anti-spoofing, audio deepfake detection, evaluation methodology, clustered bootstrap, ranking uncertainty
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
In 2021 the ASVspoof organizers tested every pair of submitted systems for significance, applied a Holm--Bonferroni correction, and published the deepfake task's systems in four groups, noting that the top two were not separated \cite{asvspoof21}. We found no uncertainty analysis in three subsequent benchmark reports: the journal overview \cite{asvspoof21journal}, ASVspoof 5 \cite{asvspoof5eval}, and the recent multi-benchmark leaderboard \cite{shim25} contain none of \emph{significance test}, \emph{confidence interval}, \emph{bootstrap}, \emph{$p$-value} or \emph{standard error}. The sub-point margins on that modern leaderboard are therefore reported without an uncertainty test; a 2026 benchmark of self-supervised models likewise ranks point EERs without an interval, error bar or test \cite{spoofsuperb26}.
The 2026 edition adds a calibration study instead; calibration establishes whether a score means what it says, not whether two systems differ.

That matters more than it would if the analyses the field did run had represented the dependence. ASVspoof~2017 reported 95\% EER confidence intervals and read their overlap \cite{delgado18}, and 2021 tested every pair \cite{asvspoof21}; both use \cite{bengio04}, and the practice then lapsed. Both variants of the cited test \cite{bengio04} treat trials within a class as independent Bernoulli draws; the dependent form accounts for two systems sharing trials, not for trials sharing speakers and attacks. ASVspoof~2021~DF contains 533,928 evaluation trials but only 93 speakers and 110 attacks, and per-attack EERs for SSL-AASIST span 0.4--10.7\%. Across the eight detectors, the one-way random-intercept ICC $\sigma_s^2/(\sigma_s^2+\sigma_e^2)$ on bona-fide scores ranges from 0.15 to 0.77, where $\sigma_s^2$ and $\sigma_e^2$ are the speaker-intercept and residual variances. ICC alone does not establish dependence in paired EER differences; that is measured directly below by matched perturbations of the joint scores and checked on two external datasets.

We audit two evaluation choices on fixed released scores: whether trials or speakers and attacks are resampled, and how observed source, speaker and attack groups are weighted. The primary question is whether pairwise separation survives those choices; we propose no new bootstrap and claim no identified population sampling law. On 21DF the six organizer-baseline pairs change from five separated to none, and on single-source SpoofCeleb the corresponding four-detector comparison changes from six to three. Alternative positive weighting rules then localize the stable conclusion: separation between the SSL-detector and organizer-baseline cohorts survives, whereas within-cohort ordering does not.

\textbf{Scope.} The primary set comprises eight score files released by the corresponding model authors or ASVspoof organizers, not the universe of public re-scores. We analyze all eleven Speech DF Arena re-scores \cite{shim25} separately. Four VCC spoof strata contain only 4/4/4/6 speakers, so no 21DF separation result transfers to new speakers or attacks. The bands and reweighting results describe fixed scores under declared procedures, not population confidence or safety.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{This benchmark's own reporting.} ASVspoof 2021 Fig.~4(c) pairs systems with the Bengio--Mari\'ethoz test \cite{bengio04} under Holm correction and publishes group structure \cite{asvspoof21}; Wang and Yamagishi \cite{wang21_comparative} showed that changing only the training seed moves ADD rankings---a variance component our intervals do \emph{not} include.

\textbf{Clustered resampling.} Poh, Martin and Bengio \cite{poh07} showed that biometric uncertainty is governed by users rather than sample count; Wu et al.\ carried user-aware resampling into speech \cite{wu17} and developed a paired correlation-corrected two-system test \cite{wu16}. Bolle et al.\ proposed a subsets bootstrap \cite{bolle04}, while Fogliato et al.\ found undercoverage for dyadic 1:1 matching \cite{fogliato24}. Our trials instead have one speaker and, for spoof, one attack: two crossed factors \cite{cgm11}, a delicate design \cite{mccullagh00,owen07} distinct from nested metrics \cite{anglin26}. Speech bootstrap intervals also include utterance- and block-based forms \cite{bisani04,liu19}. Our contribution is the audited fixed-data result: after EER threshold refitting and all-pair multiplicity are aligned, outputs change with the declared perturbation unit. We claim neither cell-level agreement with Fig.~4(c) of the ASVspoof 2021 report nor population confidence.

\textbf{Evaluation-design audits.} Zhuang et al.\ \cite{zhuang26} compute a paired-binomial detectable effect for quantization benchmarks, treating items as independent---the assumption under test here. In audio deepfakes, aggregation weights change conclusions \cite{kwok25}, channel presentation can dominate model choice \cite{delgado26presentation}, and other audits question representations \cite{benchaudit26} and corpus overlap \cite{stanek26}. None studies pairwise ranking changes under both dependence-aware resampling and alternative source/speaker/attack weights. Simultaneous rank intervals are established \cite{almohamad19}; adjacency is score-selected, so we control multiplicity over all 28 contrasts.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{EER contrast and separation event}
Two deterministic systems on a fixed trial list have no sampling uncertainty until a perturbation law is declared. Scores are oriented so larger means bona fide and sorted in ascending order. At each ordered position, bona-fide mass at or below it is the false-reject rate (FRR) and spoof mass above it is the false-accept rate (FAR); the first position minimizing $|\mathrm{FRR}-\mathrm{FAR}|$ is selected and EER is their mean, without interpolation. A distinct-score-boundary recomputation changes no displayed value by 0.001 point. For ordered pair $(A,B)$, $\Delta\mathrm{EER}_{A,B}=100(\mathrm{EER}_A-\mathrm{EER}_B)$ percentage points (negative favors $A$); every replicate refits both thresholds. A pair is \emph{separated under procedure $h$} when its simultaneous band excludes zero; write this indicator as $I_{p,h}$. The identified object is the change in $\Delta\mathrm{EER}$ and $I_{p,h}$ on the fixed scores, not population uncertainty.

\subsection{Trial and speaker$\times$attack bootstraps}
The trial bootstrap independently resamples trials within class. In the speaker$\times$attack product-weight (PW) bootstrap \cite{owen07}, speakers and spoof attacks are independently resampled with replacement; a spoof trial receives the product of its speaker and attack multiplicities, while a bona-fide trial receives only its speaker multiplicity. Both procedures use the same scores, threshold rule, $B{=}5000$ shared draws and all-pair multiplicity correction.

\subsection{All-pair multiplicity for procedure bands}
\label{sec:family}
For procedure $h$, let $s_{p,h}=\operatorname{sd}_b(\Delta_{p,h}^{(b)})$. We set $q_{.95,h}$ to the empirical 95th percentile of $\max_{p=1}^{28}|\Delta_{p,h}^{(b)}-\bar\Delta_{p,h}|/s_{p,h}$ and report $\hat\Delta_p\pm q_{.95,h}s_{p,h}$ \cite{montiel19}. This controls all 28 pairwise contrasts within each procedure. Because a population sampling law is not identified for 21DF, we call these procedure-sensitivity bands, not confidence intervals.

\textbf{Reconstructing the cited test.} Fig.~4(c) of Yamagishi et al.\ \cite{asvspoof21} does not disclose which Bengio--Mari\'ethoz variant produced its matrix, so we make no cell-level attribution. With each system's non-interpolated EER threshold selected on the evaluation set, our independent-trial and shared-trial reconstructions separate the same five of six baseline pairs. The published 34-system matrix has $\binom{34}{2}=561$ tests; even its Bonferroni cutoff is exceeded by the weakest of our five reconstructed results ($p=9.7\times10^{-6}$), while the sixth has $p=.217$. The cited derivation assumes thresholds fixed outside the test set, so these are reconstructions of its adaptation to EER rather than exact finite-sample tests.

\subsection{Alternative fixed weighting rules}
Within each class, every named hierarchy level receives equal conditional mass and trials divide their terminal-group mass equally. Bona-fide rules are empirical trial weights, equal mass over the three sources, and equal source$\rightarrow$speaker mass. Spoof rules are empirical trial weights, equal mass over five spoof strata, equal mass over observed speaker$\times$attack cells within stratum, and equal mass successively over stratum, attack family, attack and speaker. Their $3\times4$ crossing gives 12 positive, class-normalized rules fixed without inspecting scores. For endpoint $w^1$, the path from empirical weights is $w(\lambda)=(1-\lambda)w^0+\lambda w^1$, $0\leq\lambda\leq1$. We measure its distance by maximum classwise total variation, $\max_c\frac12\sum_i|w_{c,i}-w_{c,i}^0|$. Complete formulas, group maps, simulations and all 28 numerical bands are in the versioned supplement.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\sloppypar
\textbf{Setup.} The primary set contains four author-released self-supervised systems (XLSR-Mamba \cite{xiao25}, XLS-R+SLS \cite{zhang24sls}, XLSR-Conformer \cite{rosello23}, SSL-AASIST \cite{tak22}) and four organizer-released baselines (RawNet2, LFCC-LCNN, LFCC-GMM, CQCC-GMM). We use all 533,928 evaluation-phase trials for which every score is available. Table~\ref{tab:eers} reports the conventional point results. Pairwise bands use seed 20260817.
\par\endsloppypar


\textbf{Primary 21DF result} (Fig.~\ref{fig:forest}). Both reconstructions of Fig.~4(c) separate the same five of six organizer-baseline pairs; we claim no cell-level agreement with the published matrix. The matched all-28-pair bootstrap refits the same weighted EER in every replicate. Trial resampling separates 26/28 pairs and 5/6 organizer-baseline pairs; the PW bootstrap separates 18/28 and 0/6. A constrained PW bootstrap that exactly restores each of the three bona-fide source masses and five spoof-stratum masses also separates 18/28 and 0/6. This constrained arm was designed after an earlier arm failed; it is a robustness check, not prospective evidence. Thus neither threshold handling nor movement of these eight class-specific masses is necessary for the contrast. For RawNet2--CQCC-GMM, $\Delta\mathrm{EER}=-3.180$ points and the PW band is $[-9.284,2.925]$.

\textbf{Single-source SpoofCeleb check.} Repository commit \texttt{c2efa63}, dated 29 August 2026 while official access was pending, fixed the four detectors, scoring transforms, bootstrap laws, seed and six-pair endpoint before the first successful authenticated download. On all 91,130 evaluation trials \cite{jung25spoofceleb}, trial resampling separates 6/6 pairs and the PW bootstrap separates 3/6. AASIST, SLS, SSL-AASIST and XLSR-Mamba have EERs 57.93\%, 24.51\%, 26.72\% and 27.58\%; this is an off-domain sensitivity check, not a comparison of competitive SpoofCeleb systems. Every base item has one bona-fide and nine spoof renditions under one source label, so between-source mass cannot move; speaker and spoof-attack multiplicities still vary under PW draws. After observing this result, separate speaker-only and attack-only bootstraps separated 5/6 and 3/6; attack-only matched the PW indicator vector. The archived plan did not bind the scoring executable, so a later clean-source re-scoring is reproducibility evidence only: three score files were byte-identical, the fourth differed by at most $1.44\times10^{-6}$, and every EER and separation indicator was unchanged.

\textbf{What 21DF supports.} The PW bootstrap separates all 16 SSL-versus-baseline pairs, 2/6 within-SSL pairs and 0/6 within-baseline pairs. The 16 cross-cohort gaps are 19.5--23.7 points and remain separated after each leave-one-source-out refit. The two PW-separated within-SSL gaps (0.968 and 0.935 points) cease to separate after a source deletion; adjacent within-SSL gaps are only 0.032, 0.357 and 0.578 points. PW bands are $4.13$--$11.36\times$ wider than matched trial bands over all pairs (median $8.14\times$).

\textbf{Alternative weights.} The 12 fixed rules reverse 6/12 within-cohort orderings and 0/16 cross-cohort orderings; one of the 11 nonempirical linear paths reverses before maximum classwise total variation .10. After observing these outcomes, a constrained search found a positive weighting at distance .010218 that changes XLSR-Mamba minus XLS-R+SLS from $-0.032130$ to $+0.000699$ EER points. This is a found upper bound, not a minimum, and is too small to imply practical superiority. The search found no cross-cohort reversal, which is not a proof that none exists.

\textbf{Independent re-scores.} On the eleven complete Speech DF Arena score files, trial and PW bootstraps separate 52/55 and 38/55 pairs; the median pointwise-band width ratio is 8.45. These are descriptive results for that separate dataset, not population confidence intervals.

\textbf{Source deletions.} The bona-fide sources ASVspoof, VCC2018 and VCC2020 contain 67, 12 and 14 speakers. Leave-one-source-out refits preserve separation for all 16 cross-cohort pairs, remove both separated within-SSL pairs and separate three within-baseline pairs in at least one deletion. Thus cross-cohort separation survives these source changes, while every within-cohort result is conditional on them.

\textbf{ASVspoof 5 check.} For SSL-AASIST, AASIST, XLS-R+SLS and XLSR-Mamba on all 680,774 Track~1 trials \cite{asvspoof5eval}, role-stratified speaker and attack resampling removes at least one trial-bootstrap separation and gives a median simultaneous-band width ratio of $27.27\times$, exceeding the declared $2\times$ threshold. This check is intentionally EER-only because it transfers the paper's $\Delta$EER comparison; ASVspoof~5's primary Track~1 metric is minDCF. It is not a system-performance replication or population claim.

\textbf{Coverage boundary.} Under imposed bivariate random-effects models on the 21DF trial-to-group structure, the minimum low-EER coverages of the raw exact-cell inclusion--exclusion jackknife, its componentwise variance-floor version and the PW-percentile band are .845, .845 and .855; all three are below .90 in the same 11/24 low-EER cells. The complete 48-cell parameters and method formulas are in the supplement. This simulation evaluates procedures under those imposed models; it does not establish that either model describes 21DF.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

\textbf{Reporting and limits.} The complete 28-row band table, source/stratum map, weighting formulas, simulation design and artifact identifiers are in \texttt{SUPPLEMENT.md} at repository tag \texttt{m1-field-native-r2-20260830}. The legacy SSL-AASIST/AASIST files lack their originating run logs. Benchmark reports should publish trial-to-speaker/attack membership and source weights, not only trial counts. A band containing zero means the procedure does not separate that pair; it does not establish equality. We make no population, rank-confidence or formal Arena-inference claim.

\section{Conclusion}
On fixed released scores, matched 21DF bootstraps change the separated organizer-baseline count from 5/6 to 0/6, and the pre-access-archived single-source SpoofCeleb check changes it from 6/6 to 3/6. Because SpoofCeleb has one source, between-source movement cannot explain the latter contrast; an attack-only bootstrap computed after the result matches its 3/6 indicator vector. The 12 fixed weighting rules reverse 6/12 within-cohort orderings but 0/16 cross-cohort orderings. Across these rules, source deletions and searched directions, separation between the four SSL detectors and four organizer baselines is stable; within-cohort ranking is not. Population confidence requires independently sampled units and a defensible acquisition law.
